# Hooks and Scripts (reference for the ad-strategist skill)

A **hook** is the first 1–3 seconds of an ad. It has a visual part (what we see) and often a
verbal part (what we hear or read).

**A good hook:**
- works on mute (the words are on screen too),
- shows the product or the problem in the first 2 seconds,
- is about the viewer, not the brand,
- makes one promise you can keep.

---

## The 10 hook types (with fill-in formulas)

**1. Visual pattern-break: show something unexpected**
- Giant [product] standing in a real city street.
- Extreme macro of the texture, then pull back to reveal the product.
- A liquid pour freezes mid-air.
- A grey, dull scene turns warm and colourful when the product appears.

**2. Bold claim (only if you can prove it)**
- "The only [category] made with [real ingredient/feature]."
- "[Real number] customers switched this year."

**3. Question**
- "Why does your [problem] get worse by [time]?"
- "Still using [old solution]?"

**4. Call-out**
- "[Specific people], stop scrolling."
- "If you [situation], this is for you."

**5. Problem**
- "Nobody tells you this about [category]."
- "You're using [product type] wrong."

**6. Curiosity**
- "The ₹[amount] ingredient my [nani/mom] swore by…"
- "I didn't expect [product] to [result]."

**7. Transformation (honest only)**
- A before/after split screen that wipes across.
- Start with the end result, then "Let me show you how."

**8. Social proof (real numbers only)**
- "The [product] my [friend/sister] keeps stealing."
- "[Real number] five-star reviews. Here's why."

**9. Offer / urgency (real deadline only)**
- "Diwali price ends [real date]."
- "Let me save you ₹[amount]."

**10. Story / POV**
- "POV: guests arrive in 10 minutes."
- "POV: you finally found a [product] that [benefit]."

**Hinglish examples (Kesar & Co.):**
- "POV: guests 10 minute mein aa rahe hain… and your skin looks tired."
- "Mummy ne poocha, 'kya lagaya hai?' Bas ek drop."
- "Diwali ki thakaan? Ek drop. Done."

## Script structures

**6 seconds**
| Time | Part |
|---|---|
| 0–2s | Hook |
| 2–5s | Product + promise |
| 5–6s | Logo / CTA |

**15 seconds**
| Time | Part |
|---|---|
| 0–2s | Hook |
| 2–5s | Problem or desire |
| 5–10s | Product moment |
| 10–13s | Proof / benefit shown |
| 13–15s | CTA |

**30 seconds**
| Time | Part |
|---|---|
| 0–3s | Hook |
| 3–8s | Setup |
| 8–15s | The turn: product enters |
| 15–24s | Benefits shown |
| 24–27s | Proof |
| 27–30s | CTA + end card |

**Timing rule:** about 2.5 spoken words per second in English, a bit fewer in Hindi.
A 15s script is about 30–35 words.

## Example: Kesar & Co. 15s Reel (Hinglish, POV hook)

| Time | Visual | VO | On-screen text | SFX |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0–2s | Doorbell close-up, then a mirror | "POV: guests 10 minute mein…" | POV: guests in 10 min | doorbell ding |
| 2–5s | She sighs at tired skin | "…and your skin looks tired." | — | soft sigh |
| 5–10s | One golden drop on fingertips, pressed into cheeks | "One drop of Kesar & Co. Saffron Glow." | One drop. | glass tink, warm swell |
| 10–13s | Glowing skin in diya light, she smiles | "Festive glow, in one minute." | Saffron glow in 60s | sparkle |
| 13–15s | Bottle hero + logo | "Shop now." | Shop now · 15% off | logo sting |

## Storyboard frame prompt (fill-in template)

Use one per frame. Each frame becomes a start frame for a Google Flow video.

```
<Shot type> of <subject doing what>, with <the product> <where it is>.
PRODUCT LOCK: keep the exact product from the reference image: same label text, logo, shape, cap and colours. Do not redesign.
Set: <place and 2–3 props>.
Light: <direction, warmth, softness>.
Camera: <lens, angle, depth of field>.
Styling & mood: <people, clothes, feeling>.
Brand look: <paste the LOOK BLOCK · IMAGE from your Brand Bible>.
Format: <ratio>, keep the top 14% and bottom 20% clear for captions (9:16).
Avoid: text in the image, warped label, extra products, plastic skin, clutter.
```
