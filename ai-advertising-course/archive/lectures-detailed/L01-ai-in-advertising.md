## Lesson 1.1: Where AI fits in every type of ad

Say **"LESSON 1.1"** on camera to start the segment.

- **Goal:** Students see where AI replaces work, where it only helps, and where a human still has to lead.

**Talking points (in order)**
1. Advertising isn't one skill. It's about a dozen: product shots, model shoots, films, UGC, sound, editing, media buying.
2. Show the pipeline slide: **Brief → Concept → Visuals → Motion → Edit → Launch**.
3. Go step by step and say what AI does at each one:
   - Brief and Concept: **AI helps you think.** It is your research assistant and sparring partner. It doesn't decide the idea.
   - Visuals and Motion: **AI does the production.** This is where the cost and time savings come from.
   - Edit: **AI does the clicks, you give the notes.** That's vibe editing.
   - Launch: **AI multiplies.** One idea becomes 10 variations.
4. **"Anyone can press generate. Few can direct."** Feeds are full of AI ads that all look the same.
5. Introduce the 4 pillars we'll come back to in every lecture: **art direction, product accuracy, brand consistency, human touch.**
6. Say clearly where humans still win: the insight, the joke, the cultural detail, knowing when an output is wrong.
7. Introduce the demo brands: **Kesar & Co.** (hero brand, skincare), **Loom Street** (fashion), **Fizzbar** (drink).
8. **"Pick your brand today. A real one, a client's, or a made-up one. You'll use it for the whole course, and by Lecture 11 you'll have a full campaign for your portfolio."**

**Live demo**
1. Show the 12 format types from the sales page as a grid: campaign films, product ads, beauty, model and fashion, CGI, try-on hauls, GRWM and reviews, unboxing, food and beverage, beauty close-ups, luxury accessories, luxury editorials.
2. Point to each format and say which lecture covers it.

**Show on screen:** the pipeline slide, the 4-pillars slide, the 12-format grid, the demo brands' logos and palettes.

**Common mistakes to call out**
1. Opening a video tool before you have an idea. Generating isn't the same as advertising.
2. Thinking AI will make the ad "look good" on its own. Taste is still your job.
3. Using a different style for every image. A campaign needs one look.

## Lesson 1.2: Your AI ad stack and what it costs

Say **"LESSON 1.2"** on camera.

- **Goal:** Students know which tool does which job, and that they need very few tools.

**Talking points**
1. **"You don't need 30 tools. You need one for each job."**
2. Go through the stack table in `templates/ai-ad-stack.md`:
   - Video: **Google Flow** with **Google Omni Flash 1.2**.
   - Images: **Nano Banana 1.1** in Flow or Gemini, or **GPT Image 2.6** in ChatGPT.
   - Thinking, prompts and scripts: Claude, ChatGPT or Gemini, with our skills attached.
   - Voice: ElevenLabs. Music: Suno.
   - Editing: vibe editing with Claude, with CapCut as a backup.
   - Launch: Meta Ads Manager.
3. **"Models change every few months. The method doesn't. Every prompt in this course works with any model because the structure is the same."**
4. Cost: don't quote numbers from memory. Show the pricing pages live and say **"Check the current plan page. Prices change."**
5. Three budget levels to explain:
   - **Free / starter:** free tiers plus one paid tool you use the most.
   - **Freelancer:** one paid video plan plus one paid chat plan.
   - **Agency:** add paid voice and music plans for commercial use.
6. Rule of thumb: **pay for the tool that makes your final output. Use free tools for thinking and drafts.**

**Common mistakes**
1. Paying for five subscriptions in week one.
2. Burning video credits on ideas that should have been tested as still images first.

## Lesson 1.3: Setting up your tools the cheap way

Say **"LESSON 1.3"** on camera.

- **Goal:** Accounts set up, folder system made, and the first skill attached in all three chat platforms.

**Talking points**
1. Set up your folders before anything else. Show `KESAR_COURSE/` and its 6 subfolders, which match the 6 steps.
2. The file-naming rule: `BRAND_CAMPAIGN_CONCEPT_HOOK_FORMAT_RATIO_v01`. Example: `KESAR_DIWALI_GLOW_H03_UGC_9x16_v01`.
3. **"In three weeks you'll have 400 files. This naming rule is what lets you find your best ad in 10 seconds."**
4. Explain what a **skill** is: **"A skill is a file of expert instructions. Attach it to Claude, ChatGPT or Gemini, and the AI works like a specialist: it asks the right questions and gives you copy-ready prompts."**
5. **"Our skills are universal. Same file, any platform."**
6. Credit-saving habits:
   - Test ideas as still images first. Images are cheap, video is expensive.
   - Generate 2–4 options per prompt, not 20.
   - Write the prompt with a skill first, then paste it into Flow.

**Live demo: attach a skill in all three platforms (use `skills/campaign-director/SKILL.md`)**
1. **Claude:** Settings → Capabilities → Skills → upload the skill folder as a zip. Or, the simple way: drag `SKILL.md` into a chat or a Project's knowledge. Type:
   ```
   Use the campaign-director skill. My brand is Kesar & Co., a saffron face serum. Start with step 1.
   ```
2. **ChatGPT:** Explore GPTs → Create → Configure. Paste the SKILL.md text into Instructions, or upload it as a Knowledge file. Name it "Campaign Director". The quicker way: attach the .md to a normal chat and type the same message.
3. **Gemini:** Gems → New Gem. Paste the SKILL.md text into Instructions, save it. Or attach the file in a chat.
4. Show all three asking the same first questions. **"Same brain, three homes."**
5. Open `student-kit/HOW-TO-USE-SKILLS.md` and scroll so they know it's there.

**Show on screen:** the three platforms side by side, the folder structure, the naming rule in big text.

**Common mistakes**
1. Pasting only part of the skill. Always paste the whole file.
2. Starting a new chat for every step and losing the context. Use one Project, GPT or Gem per brand.
3. Leaving the default model on and wondering why the images look generic.

## Lesson 1.4: Rules, rights and AI disclosure on Meta

Say **"LESSON 1.4"** on camera.

- **Goal:** Students know what is safe to make and run, and they check every ad before launch.

**Talking points**
1. **"Meta allows AI-made ads. The rules are about honesty, not about AI."**
2. Five things to check every time:
   - **Rights:** read each tool's commercial-use terms. Some only allow commercial use on paid plans.
   - **Likeness:** no real people without written permission. No celebrities, no lookalikes, no "an actor who looks like ___".
   - **Logos and brands:** no competitor logos, no copying another brand's ad.
   - **Claims:** an AI image must not promise results the product can't deliver. Beauty before/after shots and food that looks better than the real thing are the danger zone.
   - **Disclosure:** Meta labels some AI content, and political and social-issue ads have stricter disclosure rules. In India, also check the ASCI guidelines.
3. **"I'm recording this on [today's date]. These rules change. Check the current Meta and ASCI pages before every client launch."**
4. Client work: get written permission for every product photo, and add an AI-use line to the contract (Lecture 11).
5. Fake reviews: AI UGC must not pretend to be a real customer's real experience (more in Lecture 7).

**Live demo**
1. Attach `skills/ad-policy-check/SKILL.md` in Claude.
2. Paste this test ad copy:
   ```
   Check this ad with the ad-policy-check skill.
   Brand: Kesar & Co. Saffron Glow Serum. Platform: Meta (Instagram Reels), India.
   Copy: "Remove dark spots in 3 days, guaranteed! Dermatologists' #1 choice."
   Visual: AI model, before/after split face, AI-generated.
   ```
3. Show the skill flagging "guaranteed", "3 days", "#1 choice" and the before/after, then rewriting it to something safe:
   ```
   Rewrite it so it passes, keeping the same energy.
   ```
4. Open `templates/policy-checklist.md` and show the tick boxes.

**Show on screen:** the policy check output table (✅ ⚠️ ❌), Meta's ad policy page, the ASCI site.

**Common mistakes**
1. "It's AI, so nobody can sue." Wrong. Claims law applies to every ad.
2. Using a celebrity face "just for the pitch deck".
3. Fake testimonials with invented names and invented results.
