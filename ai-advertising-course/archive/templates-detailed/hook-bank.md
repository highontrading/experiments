# Hook Bank: 120 Hook Formulas

**How to use:** A hook is the first 1–3 seconds of an ad. It has a **visual part** (what we see) and
often a **verbal part** (what we hear or read). Pick 5–6 formulas, fill the blanks for your product,
write 30 hooks, then cut to the best 5. The `hook-and-script-writer` skill does this for you and scores them.

Rules for a good hook:
- It works on mute (put the words on screen).
- It shows the product or the problem in the first 2 seconds.
- It is about the viewer, not the brand.
- It makes one promise you can keep.

---

## 1. Visual hooks (show something unexpected)
1. Product drops into frame in slow motion and lands perfectly.
2. Giant [product] standing in a real city street.
3. Extreme macro of the texture, then pull back to reveal the product.
4. A hand slams the product on the table straight at the camera.
5. Before/after split screen that wipes across.
6. The product appears from inside something unexpected (a flower, a gift box, a cloud).
7. Liquid pour or splash freezes mid-air.
8. Fast 3-cut montage: problem, problem, product.
9. Top-down flat lay that "builds itself" item by item.
10. The camera flies through a keyhole, window or box into the product scene.
11. A person stares straight into the camera, then holds up the product.
12. Colour shift: grey, dull scene turns warm and colourful when the product appears.
13. The product spins and stops with the label facing the camera.
14. Unboxing hands tear open the package in the first second.
15. Miniature people working on a giant version of the product.

## 3. Pattern-break hooks (break the scroll rhythm)
31. Start mid-sentence: "...and that's why I threw away my old serum."
32. A loud sound or snap before any word.
33. Text on screen: "Don't watch this if you love dull skin."
34. Upside-down shot that flips the right way.
35. A fake "error" or glitch, then the product appears.
36. Whisper the first line (ASMR).
37. Start with the end result, then "Let me show you how."
38. A person hushes the camera: "Shh, it's a secret."
39. A freeze-frame with a red circle around the product.
40. A wrong-format shot (old VHS, CCTV, a newspaper) that turns into a premium ad.
41. An animal or child reacting to the product.
42. A dramatic "movie trailer" voice for a simple product.
43. A countdown: "3, 2, 1..."
44. The narrator talks to the product like a person.
45. A wrong answer first: "Is it magic? No. It's saffron."
