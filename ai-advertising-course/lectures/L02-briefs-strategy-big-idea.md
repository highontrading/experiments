## Lesson 2.1: Reading a brief like an agency

Say **"LESSON 2.1"** on camera.

- **Goal:** Turn a messy client message into a one-page creative brief.

**Talking points**
1. **"Clients don't send briefs. They send WhatsApps."** Your job is to turn that into a real brief.
2. The 7 boxes of a brief (`templates/creative-brief.md`): **objective, audience, insight, single-minded message, mandatories, deliverables, KPI and deadline.**
3. Objective: say what "sales not likes" means here. It's a conversion campaign, so the KPI is purchases or cost per purchase.
4. **Single-minded message: one thing.** If an ad says three things, people remember none of them.
5. Mandatories: logo, label visible, price or offer, what the legal team has said, festive timing.
6. **"What's missing is as important as what's there."** Write a list of questions to send back to the client.

**Live demo**
1. Attach `skills/brief-and-strategy/SKILL.md` in Claude.
2. Paste the messy brief, then type:
   ```
   Decode this brief into our creative brief format. List what is missing and write 5 questions I should send the client.
   ```
3. Show the filled brief and the missing-info list (price, offer, hero claim proof, existing brand assets).
4. Answer as the client: `Price ₹899, Diwali offer: free mini with every order. Hero claim: 1% saffron extract + niacinamide for glow.`
5. Copy the final brief into `01_brief/KESAR_DIWALI_brief_v01.md`.

**Show on screen:** the WhatsApp text, then the clean one-page brief beside it.

**Common mistakes**
1. Starting visuals before the objective is clear.
2. A "message" that is a list: "glow, natural, affordable, premium, Indian".
3. Not asking for the price and offer, then making a beautiful ad nobody can act on.

**Student gets:** `student-kit/templates/creative-brief.md`, `student-kit/skills/brief-and-strategy/SKILL.md`

**Assignment:** Write your own brand's brief using the skill. Keep it to one page.

## Lesson 2.2: Audience, angle and positioning with AI

Say **"LESSON 2.2"** on camera.

- **Goal:** Find 3 sharp angles from real customer language, and fill in the Brand Bible basics.

**Talking points**
1. **"An angle is the door into the product."** The same serum can be sold as a festive glow, a 2-minute routine, a gift, or a value-for-money buy.
2. Use AI as a researcher: paste real reviews and competitor ads, and ask what people actually complain about and wish for.
3. **"Steal the words, not the ads."** The best headlines are things customers already say in reviews.
4. Audience × angle grid: 3 audiences × 3 angles = 9 options. Pick the 3 strongest.
5. Positioning line: "For [audience] who [need], Kesar & Co. is the [category] that [benefit], because [proof]."
6. The Brand Bible starts here. It's the file that keeps every image and video consistent.

**Live demo**
1. Open Meta Ad Library and search "face serum". Scroll through 5 ads and point out the patterns: the same white background and the same claims.
2. Copy 10–15 public reviews from a competitor product page into Claude:
   ```
   Using the brief-and-strategy skill: here are real reviews from a competitor serum. Find the top pains, desires and exact phrases. Then build a 3x3 audience x angle grid for Kesar & Co. Diwali and pick the 3 strongest angles with reasons.
   ```
3. Pick the angle: **"Festive glow without the parlour"**, for working women aged 24–32 in metros.
4. Attach `skills/brand-bible-builder/SKILL.md`:
   ```
   Start the brand bible for Kesar & Co. Use what we decided above. Ask me only what is missing.
   ```
5. Answer its questions using the palette: saffron #E8862A, deep red #8E1B1B, cream #F6EBD9, gold #C9A24B. Look: warm, golden hour, premium Indian, clean.

**Show on screen:** the Ad Library scroll, the 3x3 grid, the Brand Bible's first draft.

**Common mistakes**
1. "Everyone 18–45" as the audience.
2. Copying a competitor's angle so you blend in.
3. Skipping the Brand Bible and then getting 10 different styles.

## Lesson 2.3: Hooks that stop the scroll

Say **"LESSON 2.3"** on camera.

- **Goal:** Write 30 hooks, score them, and keep 5.

**Talking points**
1. **"You get about 1.5 seconds. The hook is the ad. The rest is the follow-through."**
2. The 5 hook types (`templates/hook-bank.md`):
   - **Visual hook:** something odd or beautiful in frame one (a giant serum bottle on a rooftop).
   - **Verbal hook:** the first line said or written ("Diwali party in 2 hours. No parlour slot.").
   - **Pattern break:** an unexpected cut, sound or object.
   - **Curiosity:** an open loop ("The ₹899 thing in every Diwali bag this year").
   - **Offer:** a clear deal up front ("Free mini with every order. Till Diwali only.").
3. A good hook has both a visual and a verbal part. Plan them together.
4. Score each hook on 3 things: **Stops the scroll? Clear in 1 second? Leads to the product?**
5. **"Hinglish is allowed. Write how your audience talks, not how a textbook talks."**

**Live demo**
1. Attach `skills/hook-and-script-writer/SKILL.md`:
   ```
   Write 30 hooks for Kesar & Co. Diwali, angle "festive glow without the parlour". Mix all 5 hook types, English and Hinglish. Each hook = visual + on-screen text + first spoken line. Score them and give me the top 5.
   ```
2. Read the top 5 aloud. Reject one live and explain why (a vague hook like "Glow up this Diwali").
3. Save the top 5 as H01–H05. These IDs go into the file names later.

**Show on screen:** the hook table with scores, the 5 hook types as an on-screen list.

**Common mistakes**
1. Starting with the logo. Nobody cares yet.
2. Hooks that need context to make sense.
3. A great hook that has nothing to do with the product (clickbait that doesn't lead to a sale).

**Student gets:** `student-kit/templates/hook-bank.md`, `student-kit/skills/hook-and-script-writer/SKILL.md`

**Assignment:** Generate 30 hooks for your brand, keep 5, and label them H01–H05.

## Lesson 2.4: Writing scripts and storyboards

Say **"LESSON 2.4"** on camera.

- **Goal:** A 15s script and a 6-frame AI storyboard you'll use in the video lectures.

**Talking points**
1. The 15s structure: **Hook (0–2s) → Problem/desire (2–5s) → Product moment (5–10s) → Proof/benefit (10–13s) → CTA (13–15s).**
2. The 30s version adds a mini-story or a second proof. Write the 30s first and the 15s is easy to cut.
3. **"Write for sound off."** On-screen text carries the message. Voice is a bonus.
4. Storyboard = 6 frames. Each frame has a shot type, an action, on-screen text and VO.
5. Storyboard frames are cheap AI images. **"Fix the story at the image stage. Fixing it at the video stage costs 10 times more."**

**Live demo**
1. Same chat, `hook-and-script-writer`:
   ```
   Write a 15s and a 30s script for hook H02. Then make a 6-frame storyboard table: shot, action, on-screen text, VO, and an image prompt for each frame using the image prompt formula. Format 9:16. Use our Kesar brand bible.
   ```
2. Copy frame 1's image prompt into Flow → Nano Banana 1.1. Example of what it should look like:
   ```
   SUBJECT: a young Indian woman, 27, in a cream cotton kurta, looking at her watch in a softly lit bedroom, Diwali diyas on the window sill.
   PRODUCT LOCK: Kesar & Co. serum bottle on the dresser, exact product from the reference image: same label text, logo, shape, gold cap, colours; do not redesign.
   SET: modern Indian apartment bedroom, wooden dresser, marigold garland.
   LIGHT: warm golden evening light from the window, soft diya glow.
   CAMERA/LENS: medium shot, 35mm, eye level, shallow depth of field.
   STYLING & MOOD: rushed but hopeful, festive.
   BRAND LOOK: saffron, deep red, cream and gold palette; warm, premium Indian, clean.
   FORMAT: 9:16 vertical, photoreal.
   Avoid: extra text, distorted hands, a different bottle shape, skin lightening.
   ```
3. Generate 2 options, pick one. Show the 6 pre-generated frames as a grid.
4. Put the frames into `templates/script-storyboard.md`.

**Show on screen:** the script timing bar, the 6-frame storyboard grid.

**Common mistakes**
1. The product only appears in the last second.
2. VO and on-screen text saying the same words. Make them work together instead.
3. Writing 20 seconds of words for a 15s ad.

**Student gets:** `student-kit/templates/script-storyboard.md`, `student-kit/prompt-library/images.md`

**Assignment:** Write a 15s script and make 6 storyboard frames for your best hook.
