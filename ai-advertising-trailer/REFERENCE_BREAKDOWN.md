# Reference breakdown: "AI Masterclass — pick your side" (56 s)

Built from all 1,678 frames read at the source's 29.97 fps, a word-level transcript, a
stem split of the soundtrack (voice, drums, bass and other), spectrograms, and beat and loudness analysis.
The keyframe sheet is `reference/keyframes.jpg`.

**Spec:** 1280×720 (probably a 1080p master), 29.97 fps, 55.99 s, AAC stereo. Loudness is
**−11.6 LUFS integrated**, which is loud social-media mastering.

---

## 1. The idea in one line

A manifesto about history repeating itself. *Every era gets a force (fire, electricity, the
internet), and people split into those who cross over and those who don't. AI is that force
now. Which side are you on?* The ad sells a mindset, not a product. The brand appears only
in the last 3 seconds.

## 2. Structure: five acts, each with its own look

| Act | Time | Look | Job |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Pattern | 0:00–0:04.4 | 1:1 square window, strobing match-cuts | Hook: "Human progress has always followed the same pattern." |
| 2. History | 0:04.4–0:15.3 | Black, film-scan frames, collage | Fire, electricity, internet. Some people cling to the old world. |
| 3. The new world | 0:15.3–0:25.8 | **Pure white**, black type, AI logos | Others wake up and move. "AI is that force." |
| 4. The gap | 0:25.8–0:32.6 | Black, glowing orbs | Shapers vs. the shaped. "Compounding every single day." |
| 5. Proof | 0:32.6–0:48.1 | Film-frame interview clips | Three authority soundbites (Hinton, Ng, Arjun Jain) |
| 6. Ultimatum + brand | 0:48.1–0:56 | Black, neon fork lines, then the end card | "Two futures are forming… which side are you on?", then the logo and URL |

The act breaks are marked by the same **light-leak flash** each time (4.4 s, 32.6 s, 48.1 s).
Black means the past and the stakes. White means the new world. Film texture means real people
and real history.

## 3. Shot-by-shot (frame numbers at 29.97 fps)

### Act 1: the match-cut strobe (f0–f132)
- **f0–f87 (0–2.9 s):** a centred **720×720 square** with black pillarboxes. **About 45 different
  images in 3 seconds**, a new one every 1–2 frames (33–67 ms). Every image is a **circular object
  centred on the same point**, so the eye never moves: pilot helmets, basketball, beach ball,
  Buddha halo, manhole covers (×5), life ring, bicycle wheels, jet engine, round windows, clock
  faces (×4), gong, temple wheel, domes and ceilings (×5), chandelier. These morph into **cloud spirals
  and hurricanes (f55–f83)**, which become a satellite **Earth that zooms out and rotates to the night
  side (f84–f91)**, then fade to black.
- **Caption:** one word at a time, dead centre, white, *Human / progress / has / always / followed /
  the / same / pattern.* Each word swaps on the frame its voiceover word starts. The images
  don't care about the words; they just keep strobing.
- **f96–f132 (3.2–4.4 s):** black. "A force appears" builds word by word. Each new word **rises
  from about 12 px below the baseline and eases up over about 6 frames**, so for a moment the
  words sit at staggered heights.
- **f133–f143:** a **light-leak / film-burn flash**. A soft white wedge with an orange and cyan
  chromatic edge sweeps in from the lower left, fills the frame white in 4 frames, holds for
  6 frames, then hard-cuts.

### Act 2: history (f143–f467)
- **f143–f247: "fire / electricity / internet."** Each shot sits inside a **scanned film frame**:
  torn and ragged edges, a rounded **sprocket hole outline on the left**, a thin rebate line on
  the right, occasional *KODAK* edge-print flicker, and slight gate weave. The picture fills
  about 58% of the width on black, with a slow push-in. Cavemen making fire (AI-generated), an
  Edison-type figure whose **bulb switches on at f178**, then sepia 1970s computer-lab photos
  (3 shots cut at f211, f215 and f219). About one second per era, cut on the beat.
- **f248–f337:** black, rising words: "And every time." then "Not everyone crosses over." Long
  words are split at syllables to follow the voice ("ti" + "me.", "si" + "ng" + "le").
- **f338–f399: "Some people carry the old world with them."** An **old-world collage builds
  card by card** (hard pop-ins, no animation), stacked and overlapping in the centre:
  typewriter, library, BlackBerry, 1990 Mac, cassette boombox, record player.
- **f400–f431: "like it still makes sense."** The collage **explodes outward**. Every card
  flies radially, rotates ±10–20° and eases out, then keeps drifting slowly.
- **f432–f458:** a **small glowing white orb** appears under the caption and floats up to the
  centre, growing. "Others" sits inside it, and the text inverts with a difference blend.
- **f458–f467:** the orb **scales up hard (ease-in) and fills the screen white**. This circle
  wipe is the door into the new world.

### Act 3: the new world (f467–f773), all on white
- Black text on white. "wake / up / inside" hard-swap word by word.
- **f478–f500: AI app icons pop in** (scale 0 → 110% → 100%, staggered by about 1 frame) in a
  ring around the word "new / one": Claude, a Grok-style black badge, a lime squiggle app, a
  red mascot, OpenAI and Gemini.
- **f501–f520: "and move."** The whole layout **whip-pans right** with heavy horizontal motion
  blur (about 10 frames). "move." **fades in from light grey to black**.
- **f529–f555: "No announcement"** fades in, then holds.
- **f556–f714:** a **collage of founder archive photos on white** builds card by card on the
  words (Gates, Jobs, Wozniak, Dell, Ellison and others in the 70s and 80s). Cards are swapped
  in place as the line goes on: "No one handed them a roadmap. They moved before the map
  existed. Right now…"
- **f715–f773:** an empty white screen. "AI" fades in (grey → black), then "is", "that", "force",
  "and the", each with an opacity fade of about 6 frames.

### Act 4: the gap (f773–f975), back to black
- **f773–f790: "gap", the signature transition.** A **black vertical bar opens from the middle of
  the word "gap"**, splitting it into "ga | p", and widens to fill the screen (ease-out, about
  10 frames). The leftover white slivers slide off left and right, and the next word "it's"
  arrives with them. The word itself becomes the transition.
- **f790–f831:** "creating / between" in white on black. A tiny white dot ignites on the word and
  grows into a **glowing orb** behind the text (difference blend, so the text turns black inside it).
- **f832–f895: "those who shape the intelligence / and those shaped by it."** **Eight satellite orbs
  fly in from off-screen** into a ring around the centre orb. The ring **rotates clockwise and
  contracts**, and the orbs merge into the centre, which **shrinks to nothing** on "by it".
- **f914–f975:** rising words: "is / compounding / every / single / day."
- **f976–f985:** the same light-leak flash to white.

### Act 5: proof (f986–f1440)
- Interview clips inside the **same film-frame overlay** (torn edges, sprocket, jitter), with a
  slow continuous push-in.
- **Lower third:** the name in a bold geometric sans (Poppins Bold style), plus two small regular
  lines of credentials. The original broadcaster's bug is left visible.
- Hinton segment (f986–f1150): interviewer question, then answer. Andrew Ng (f1151–f1318). Dr. Arjun
  Jain (f1319–f1440). Cuts happen only where the speakers change. No captions in this act.
- **f1441–f1450:** the light-leak flash to white, then a hard cut to black.

### Act 6: ultimatum and end card (f1451–f1677)
- **f1451–f1595: "Two futures are forming. The line is being drawn. Do you know which side you're
  on?"** Words fade in. **Two glowing neon lines** (white with a soft bloom) draw in from the left
  as trim paths. On "forming" they **fork**: the top line steps up and the bottom line steps down.
  They keep drawing off to the right. From "which side" they **retract** (the trim end runs left
  to right) and are gone by "on?".
- **f1596–f1627:** the end card, **in silence**. A logo in a pixel/techno display font sits above
  an outlined pill button, "pick your side", in a monospace font. It is revealed by a
  left-to-right wipe over about 15 frames, with two glitch ticks.
- **f1628–f1677:** a hard swap to the URL in monospace, which holds until the end.

## 4. Design system

- **Colour:** only pure black and pure white. All colour comes from footage. The black-to-white
  flips carry the story.
- **Type:** one face for the whole voiceover, a tight grotesk (Helvetica Neue / Inter Bold at
  −2% tracking, about 5% of frame height). It is always centred and shows 1–4 words at a time,
  locked to the voiceover word timings to within about 1 frame. The brand only gets its own fonts
  on the end card.
- **Three text entrances, and no exits** (words simply cut):
  1. **hard swap** per word (montages)
  2. **rise from baseline** per word (black sections)
  3. **opacity fade** from grey (white sections and the finale)
- **Text over the orbs uses a difference blend.**
- **Transitions, in order of use:** hard cut on the beat, then the light-leak flash ×3 (act
  breaks only), circle-grow wipe, whip-pan with motion blur, the split-door "gap" (black bar
  from the centre of a word), and cut to black.
- **Motion:** a slow push-in on every piece of footage, collage pop-in then radial scatter,
  orbit-and-collapse, neon trim-path lines. Every move is an ease-out, with no linear moves.
- **Texture:** the film-scan frame is used only for real-world footage (history and people),
  never on graphics.
- **Pacing:** 0.03–0.07 s per shot in the hook, about 1 s per shot in act 2, and 3–5 s holds per
  interview. The edit speeds up and slows down with the music.

## 5. Sound design

**Voiceover:** a deep male voice (median pitch 96 Hz, range 78–133 Hz), close-mic'd and dry. The
delivery is slow and documentary-like, about 140 wpm, with long deliberate pauses. Every cut and
caption hangs off it.

**Music:** a cinematic hybrid of an 808 trap pulse and orchestral pads. It runs at about **72 BPM
half-time** (librosa reads 143 BPM), in **F# minor**. Its arc:

| Time | What happens |
|---|---|
| 0–3.0 | Sustained choir/organ-like pad with a **crescendo riser** (2.0–3.0), plus tiny **tick transients on the strobe cuts** |
| 3.0–4.4 | **Near silence** under "A force appears" (a faint low tone only), with a whoosh swell starting at 4.0 |
| 4.45 | **DROP:** a whoosh into a **sub-bass boom** on the light-leak |
| 4.4–32.5 | Groove: 808 sub notes with pitch glides, sparse kick, plucked melodic line around 1 kHz |
| 13.6–15.3 | Mini-break: drums thin out, the highs drop away, then **sine "ping" shimmers (3.5 kHz and 9 kHz)** as the orb appears, and a **whoosh swell** while the orb fills the screen |
| 32.6–45.5 | **Drums and bass drop out completely**, leaving only a soft pad bed under the interviews |
| 45.5 | The sub-bass swell returns under the last soundbite |
| 48.1–53.0 | Finale build: **noise riser and an accelerating hi-hat roll**, rising in level |
| **53.0** | **Hard stop on "on?"** with no tail |
| 53.0–56 | The end card plays in silence apart from **two glitch ticks** (53.7 s and 54.1 s) on the logo reveal |

**SFX inventory:** cut ticks in the strobe, a whoosh and sub boom on each light-leak, a
shimmer ping for the orb's birth, a whoosh for the circle wipe, a swoosh for the whip-pan, sub
drops, a riser and hat roll for the finale, and glitch ticks on the logo.

**Mix:** the voice sits 6–10 dB above the music, and the music ducks further under the
interviews. Master at −11.6 LUFS.

## 6. Why it works (what to keep)

1. **One idea, and the whole visual system serves it.** Circles in the hook, then the orb (a
   new force), a ring (shapers), and a fork (two futures). The shapes themselves tell the story.
2. **The words drive the picture.** Every caption, cut and animation lands on a voiceover word.
3. **Contrast is the transition.** Black/white flips and three identical light-leaks act as
   chapter markers.
4. **Silence as punctuation:** before the drop (3.0–4.4 s) and after the last word.
5. **The brand only at the end**, so it feels like a film, not an ad.

## 7. What not to copy

- The hook images, the founder photos and the broadcast interview clips are third-party
  footage. For our channel we use our own studio work, licensed stock, or generated images and
  footage, and no broadcaster clips or bugs.
- The voice, music, logo fonts and copy are recreated in the same style, never lifted.
