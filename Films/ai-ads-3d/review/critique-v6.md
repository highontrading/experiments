# Critique: draft-v6

1. **43.0–44.5 s · camera goes through the set.** As the camera leaves the "ORDER PLACED for AHMET" phone, the frame at 43 s is almost all empty beige with a phone corner at the top. The camera then drops through the green bag and the steel rail of the carousel and ends up skimming the terrazzo floor, with a see-through pane over it, before the boxes come into view. A viewer reads this as the camera clipping through objects. **Fix:** end the phone move by rising and pulling back over the bag, not diving down. Keep the camera above the top of the rail and clear of the bag until the boxes on the feed belt are in frame.

2. **37.5–43 s · flat void around the phone.** The camera pushes into the green bag until it fills the frame, then lands on a phone lying on a plain, evenly lit beige surface. With no bag edge, handle, belt or light falloff in view, the surface reads as an empty CG plane, not the top of a suitcase. It is the oldest-looking CG in the film. **Fix:** frame wider, so a bag seam or handle and a strip of the moving belt stay in view. Give the bag top a fabric or leather texture and some shading, and come down onto it from above, not through its side.

3. **55–63 s · the ending loses AHMET.** On the help desk monitor the advert is a small yellow card, about 60 px wide at 960×540, and the name AHMET cannot be read, least of all on a phone. As the camera drifts, the BAGGAGE SERVICES sign is cut off at the top of frame (60–62 s). The page at 53.4 s says "the person it was written for" without a name, so the payoff that it is Ahmet who never comes rests on type nobody can see. **Fix:** keep pushing in until the monitor fills about a third of the frame with AHMET readable, and the empty chair beside it, behind the desk, is in shot. Either keep the sign whole or frame it out completely. Also consider naming him in the page ("…passenger Ahmet, please come to the Baggage Services desk").
